Research Work

Central Theme of my Research Work

My research work lies at the intersection of machine learning, biomedical signal processing, and applied data engineering. I have worked extensively on EEG, ECG, and PPG signals for problems such as sleep stage classification, sleep apnea detection, and anomaly detection. My work focuses on building end-to-end pipelines — from signal preprocessing and feature learning to model evaluation and deployment readiness. I have implemented and compared classical machine learning models with deep learning architectures including CNNs, temporal models, and Variational Autoencoders, with an emphasis on interpretability, robustness, and real-world usability. Beyond model accuracy, my research prioritizes reproducibility, clean experimentation, and translating research ideas into practical, deployable systems.

Patent: APNEATIC EYE

Apneatic Eye is a research-driven wearable concept focused on early detection and monitoring of sleep apnea using non-invasive physiological signals. The system explores the use of eye-area sensing combined with signals such as nasal-breathing pressure oxygen saturation and bio-signals to identify apnea events in real time. Designed with both clinical relevance and user comfort in mind, Apneatic Eye aims to bridge the gap between laboratory-grade sleep studies and accessible, everyday monitoring. The project emphasizes signal reliability, intelligent anomaly detection, and practical feedback mechanisms to support timely intervention and improved sleep health.
